# Editorial policy

## Research before scripting

Maintain a claim ledger for every episode: claim ID, claim text, source title, author or institution, URL or bibliographic reference, page or passage, evidence type, confidence, and reviewer notes. Scripts and scene plans reference claim IDs.

Prefer inscriptions and archaeological reports with scholarly interpretation, museum and archive collections, and established academic publications. Use modern explainers to discover sources, then verify their underlying evidence. Search snippets and model output are not historical evidence.

Separate established evidence, scholarly interpretations, disputed claims, and literary or religious traditions. Attribute uncertainty in Tamil narration. Seek independent corroboration for consequential or disputed claims; repeated copies of the same source are not independent evidence. Omit claims that cannot be supported.

Do not invent quotations, precise dates, etymologies, borders, or biographical details. A literary work can support discussion of that work without automatically proving that an event happened as described.

## Tamil scripts and narration

- Draft in natural spoken Tamil; avoid unnecessarily literal translations from English.
- Keep a glossary for historical names, place names, dates, and pronunciation notes.
- Review a short voice sample containing difficult names before generating full narration.
- Listen to the complete narration and check captions against it.
- Measure duration from rendered audio. Adjust scripts and pacing to approach ten minutes without filler.

## Visuals and audio

Record the origin, license, attribution requirements, and usage rights for each external asset. Track generated assets separately. Label reconstructions and illustrative AI imagery so viewers do not mistake them for archival evidence. Verify maps, scripts, clothing, and architecture against the period being discussed.

Use music with documented rights and keep it below narration. Check Tamil font rendering, text wrapping, and caption timing in both video layouts.

## Publication review

Review sources, pronunciation, visual accuracy, captions, attribution, and title/thumbnail claims. Prepare source references for the description. Recheck current platform disclosure and upload requirements when implementing publishing. A failed review returns the affected stage for revision rather than regenerating the entire project.
